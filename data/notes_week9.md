# Week 9 — a reason for every game

Researched 27 October. The two numbers per game (sportsbook spread and
FPI) are in `data/opinions.tsv`. This is what is NOT in either of them.

**The lesson of the week, about my own model:** FPI does not know about
injuries. It rates a team on what it has already done, so wherever the
news has changed who is playing, FPI is stale and the market is not.
Four games are market-only below for exactly that reason.

## Thursday and Friday — these lock at kickoff, not Saturday noon

**1. CAR @ GB — Green Bay.** GB -3.5 at home, total 47.5. Both sides are
hurt on a short week. Green Bay is missing Micah Parsons (PUP), Zaire
Franklin (hairline shin fracture) and Sean Rhyan (high ankle); Carolina
is worse off up front, with right tackle Taylor Moton on IR, left guard
Damien Lewis's knee, and Jaelan Phillips questionable. Covers leans
Carolina with the points. Take Green Bay, expect it close and low.

**2. BAY @ UCF — BAYLOR.** My first note here was a slogan: "take the
side that is not in upheaval". It did not survive looking at.

The narrative is loud. UCF beat #2 BYU 39-31 and the fans stormed the
field; Baylor lost 25-24 at Kansas and fired Dave Aranda two days
later at 40-40, with defensive coordinator Joe Klanderman taking over
and keeping the defensive play-calling. On that story this is not a
hard pick.

Underneath it, the two teams are the same team. Both 4-3, both 2-2 in
the Big 12. UCF's win was a double-overtime eight-point margin — one
possession from being a loss. Baylor's loss was by one, and they put
55 on Arizona State earlier this year. FPI, which knows nothing about
sacked coaches or storming fields, has it 50.3 to 49.7.

The market has read the same story and cannot pick a side: Yahoo's
consensus has Baylor -1.5, SportsLine has UCF -1.5, ESPN's number has
UCF by about a point. When the books disagree on who is favoured, the
game really is a coin flip and the narrative is already spent.

Two situational arguments exist and they point the same way, against
the story. UCF is in a field-storm letdown spot on a short Friday
turnaround — three analysts on record taking Baylor with the points
for exactly that reason. And teams usually play hard in the first game
after a mid-season firing, so "Baylor is in chaos" may have it
backwards.

Taken as the cheapest deviation on the card: about three points of win
probability, against twenty-eight for Clemson. Not because Baylor is
better. They are not, and neither is UCF.

One unknown at the time of writing: DJ Lagway has had a high ankle
sprain since the opener, is week to week, has already missed two games
and is completing 52.1%. No availability report had been posted.

## The one deviation

Twenty-nine of thirty are the market favourite. That is not a strategy,
it is what happens when every piece of news turns out to be already in
the price — Sayin, Hurts, Houser, Jackson, Baylor's coach, all of it was
priced before I looked. Research made the card correct; it did not make
it different.

Games 2, 5 and 6 are the exceptions. Clemson was taken from a list of
five defensible deviations; Wisconsin was his own, and it was right —
my note on that game had understated it by quoting a two-week-old stat
and omitting both teams' most recent results. Baylor came out of his
own observation that the obvious read (one team lost and fired its
coach, one beat the number two side) ought to make it an easy pick,
which is exactly the tell: the market has priced that story and still
cannot choose a side, so the story is spent and the game is free
separation. Arizona came last and is the best of the three; Clemson
was dropped to make room for it, because three deviations with real
arguments beats four where one is only an argument about a price.

Still on the table and declined: Cleveland at Pittsburgh (CBS picks
Cleveland outright), Arizona at Texas Tech (Tech lost to unranked
Cincinnati, Arizona won 39-3), Chicago at Seattle (the 5-2 side is the
underdog) and Atlanta at Tampa Bay (Tampa is 2-5).

**A process note, from getting Wisconsin wrong.** Both relevant results
were sitting in the game table and I trusted a search summary's stat
instead. Last week's scores are the cheapest form check available and
they are already local. Read them first, every week, before quoting
anyone's recent form.

## Where I was wrong this week

**CONN @ AFA was my bug, not the app's.** The spread was read off a
printed string and matched against ESPN's spelling of the home team;
when the spelling did not match, the code took the line for the away
side and flipped the sign. Fixed, and it now declines to guess.

**Kansas State was not a second instance of it.** I said it was. The
source wrote the same line from the underdog's side ("Colorado +2.5")
and I read it as the opposite. The code had that one right.

# Wednesday 28 October — what moved

Re-priced all thirty. **No favourite changed on any game**, so the card
stands. Nine lines moved; three of them say something.

**BAL @ BUF — the market is pricing Lamar in.** Buffalo 60.5% to 59.0%,
moving toward Baltimore. Jackson practised Wednesday with the left
ankle taped, warming up with the starting centre; Rapoport has him
"believed to be close to 100 percent", Minter still will not commit and
has him questionable. So the edge on Buffalo is shrinking exactly as
you would expect if he plays. Still Buffalo, by less.

**KSU @ COLO — Kansas State is barely a favourite now.** 56.0% to 53.6%,
drifting to Colorado. Avery Johnson is still only day-to-day and Klein
has not disclosed the injury; the Big 12 availability report lands
Friday. This was already flagged as the likeliest pick to go wrong and
the market is edging that way too.

**CONN @ AFA — nearly a coin flip.** Air Force 56.0% to 53.6%. Covers
had already flagged Evan Medders as doubtful for Air Force. Still Air
Force, but it has thinned.

**Thursday's game, which locks at kickoff.** Final reports are in and
both sides are hurt. Green Bay rules out centre Sean Rhyan (ankle),
right guard Travis Glover (knee) and linebacker Zaire Franklin (shin),
with Micah Parsons already on PUP and Matthew Golden questionable —
that is two of five offensive line spots, both hurt in Sunday's loss at
Detroit. Carolina rules out its best pass rusher Jaelan Phillips
(hamstring), left guard Damien Lewis (knee) and corner Akayleb Evans.
A patchwork Green Bay interior against a Carolina front missing its
edge largely cancels. Line unmoved at Green Bay -3.5. Pick stands.

**Ohio State still has not named a quarterback.** Day declined again,
wants to see a couple more practices, and says it is "definitely

expectation. The line has not moved, so the market is not worried.

Unchanged and still the two deviations: Wisconsin at Iowa, Clemson at
Florida State.


## Thursday night, final call — GREEN BAY

Yesterday I wrote that the two injury lists "roughly cancel". That was
wrong, and in Green Bay's favour. Carolina's absences land exactly on
the weakness they already had.

Carolina lost 43-13 to Tampa Bay in their last game. Bryce Young was
sacked five times, threw two interceptions and fumbled — one of the
worst starts of his career. Right tackle Taylor Moton fractured his
fibula in it and rookie Monroe Freeling is filling in; left guard
Damien Lewis is out with a knee; their best pass rusher Jaelan
Phillips is out with a hamstring. So a quarterback who was just
pressured into a five-sack afternoon goes to Lambeau on a short week
behind a patched line, with no edge rush of his own to make Green Bay
sit back.

Green Bay is missing its centre Sean Rhyan and right guard Travis
Glover, both hurt in Sunday's loss at Detroit, with Micah Parsons
already on PUP and Matthew Golden questionable. That is real, but it
costs them interior push, not their quarterback, and Lukas Van Ness is
still coming off the edge at a patchwork tackle.

Weather is a non-factor: clear, about 56F, light wind.

Market has Green Bay at 60.5% and the line has not moved all week. If
anything this looks a touch low. **Take Green Bay.**

One caveat recorded honestly: one outlet reported Moton returning for
this game, which contradicts the fibula fracture. The fracture report
is the better sourced of the two, and the rest of the case does not
rest on him.
